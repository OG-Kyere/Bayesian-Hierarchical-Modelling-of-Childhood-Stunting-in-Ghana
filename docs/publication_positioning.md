# Publication positioning: bounded claims

This is a targeted positioning check, not a systematic review or proof of novelty.

| Closest study | Verified overlap | Implication for this project |
|---|---|---|
| Aheto et al. (2015), *Paediatric and Perinatal Epidemiology*, DOI [10.1111/ppe.12222](https://doi.org/10.1111/ppe.12222) | Analysed 2,083 children nested in 1,641 households using multilevel regression and discussed unexplained household variation. | Household clustering and residual household heterogeneity are established Ghana research topics; do not claim to introduce them. |
| Iddrisu & Gyabaah (2023), *BMJ Open*, [article e075723](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/13/12/e075723) | Bayesian multilevel ordinal analysis of Ghana MICS 2017–2018; explicitly acknowledges children within households within higher clusters. | Neither Bayesian multilevel modelling nor recognition of this hierarchy is novel in Ghana. Binary stunting and a newer DHS distinguish scope, but do not by themselves establish a publication contribution. |
| Other Ghana studies already cited in the report | Bayesian geostatistical and geo-additive studies, recent DHS inequality analyses, and WASH research overlap with the substantive topic. | Keep the nonspatial scope explicit; spatial studies are relevant comparators, not components of this analysis. |

The defensible question to evaluate is whether the **household/community variance contrast and selected conditional associations remain stable across sample harmonization, household clustering, prior specification, and survey-weight sensitivity**, with transparent reproducibility and full diagnostics. This is a proposed contribution, not a verified first-in-literature claim.

Before journal submission, expand the comparison matrix from full-text review, including each study's outcome construction, hierarchy actually fitted, handling of survey weights, maternal-information selection, and variance sensitivity checks. Full-text retrieval was not available for every comparator in this repair session; absence of a feature from an abstract must not be interpreted as absence from the study.

The cross-sectional analysis does not identify causal effects, the primary posterior is not fully survey-design calibrated, and conditional WAIC is not evidence of prediction in new communities. These limitations belong in the final article rather than only in supplementary code documentation.
