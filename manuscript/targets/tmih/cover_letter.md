Please consider the manuscript, “Household and Community Heterogeneity in Childhood Stunting in Ghana,” for publication as a Research Article in *Tropical Medicine & International Health*.

This cross-sectional secondary analysis addresses a focused public-health question: once children living in the same household are allowed to share a household-level random effect, how much residual variation in stunting remains at the survey-community level?

Using the 2022 Ghana Demographic and Health Survey, the study includes 4,928 children nested within 3,545 households and 616 survey communities. The residual household component was substantially larger than the community component in the final Bayesian hierarchical model and remained so after adjustment for child characteristics, household wealth, region, residence, WASH, and maternal education. The manuscript also reports prespecified robustness work using alternative priors, survey-weight sensitivity, missing-data treatment, spline age specification, detailed WASH coding, posterior predictive checks, PSIS-LOO, and independent GEE analyses. The interpretation remains explicitly non-causal.

The manuscript fits the journal’s public-health and low- and middle-income-country scope, with particular relevance to childhood growth, socioeconomic inequality, household context, and water and sanitation. Its central contribution is the separation of household from community heterogeneity rather than the presentation of another list of stunting correlates.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. The DHS microdata are restricted and are not redistributed. Eligible researchers may request access from The DHS Program, while the public project repository provides the analysis code and aggregate, non-identifying outputs needed to inspect the workflow.
