Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “Household and Community Heterogeneity in Childhood Stunting in Ghana: A Bayesian Hierarchical Analysis of the 2022 Demographic and Health Survey,” for publication as a Research Article in *Maternal & Child Nutrition*.

The paper grew out of a simple question that was not answered well by a community-only model: once children in the same household are allowed to share a household-level random effect, how much residual variation in stunting is still left at the community level?

Using the 2022 Ghana Demographic and Health Survey, we analysed 4,928 children nested within 3,545 households and 616 survey communities. The household component was consistently much larger than the community component. That pattern remained after adding drinking-water source, sanitation, maternal education, household wealth, region, residence, age, and sex. We also tested the stability of the findings using alternative priors, survey-weight sensitivity, missing-data sensitivity, a spline representation of age, more detailed WASH coding, posterior predictive checks, and PSIS-LOO.

We think the manuscript will be of interest to the journal because it treats household context as part of the substantive nutrition question rather than only as a statistical nuisance. It also uses the most recent nationally representative Ghana DHS and shows that the remaining burden of stunting is not fully explained by the standard socioeconomic and environmental measures available in the survey.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. Restricted DHS microdata are not included in the public repository; eligible researchers can request access directly from The DHS Program. Analysis code, aggregate tables, figures, and non-identifying diagnostics are available for reproducibility.
