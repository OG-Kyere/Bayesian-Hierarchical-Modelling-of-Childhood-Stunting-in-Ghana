\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{singlespace}
Childhood stunting remains an important public-health concern in Ghana despite substantial national progress. This study investigated individual-, household-, and community-level factors associated with stunting among children under five using the 2022 Ghana Demographic and Health Survey and Bayesian hierarchical logistic regression. The analytic sample comprised 4,928 children with valid height-for-age measurements nested within 3,545 households and 616 survey communities. Survey-weighted stunting prevalence was 17.4\% (95\% confidence interval: 15.8\%--18.9\%). Sequential models introduced household and community random intercepts, water and sanitation characteristics, and maternal education, with prior, survey-weight, missing-data, posterior-predictive, and model-comparison sensitivity analyses. In the household-and-community model, the posterior median household standard deviation was 1.07 compared with 0.42 at the community level; the household variance partition coefficient was approximately 0.25 and the community intraclass correlation approximately 0.04, indicating substantially greater residual heterogeneity between households. Boys had higher posterior odds of stunting than girls, while children aged 24--35 months had the highest age-related odds. A strong household-wealth gradient persisted after adjustment. In the fully adjusted maternal-education model, unimproved drinking-water source was associated with higher posterior odds of stunting (odds ratio 1.50, 95\% posterior interval: 1.14--2.00), whereas the sanitation association was weaker and uncertain (1.11, 0.87--1.43). Higher maternal education was associated with substantially lower posterior odds of stunting relative to no formal education (0.38, 0.19--0.70). Posterior predictive checks reproduced overall and age-specific prevalence patterns reasonably well, and the harmonized WASH and maternal-education models showed similar predictive performance. The findings indicate that Ghana's remaining stunting burden is strongly patterned by household socioeconomic and environmental conditions and that substantial unmeasured household-level heterogeneity remains after standard DHS covariates are included.
\end{singlespace}
